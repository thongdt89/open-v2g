[x] TASK-1 | P1 | Stop active charging when HLC event overflow revokes permission | done
Status: FIXED — implementation 91cd37030d2af3c1c0a28616561ad34415e24704
Review finding: 1
Primary file: src/charging_coordinator/charging_coordinator.c
Operations: MarkHlcEventOverflow, EvaluatePermission, Tick

Problem:
  MarkHlcEventOverflow revokes permission and requires status resynchronization
  without starting a hardware safe-state/stop transaction. EvaluatePermission
  returns early during resynchronization; Tick only checks permission expiry
  while ALLOW. Simulated hardware can therefore remain ENERGIZED under DENY.

Reproduction:
  Establish energy transfer at time 160. Mark current-generation HLC overflow
  at 170, evaluate permission at 171, then Tick(10000000). At the review
  baseline, permission is DENY, hardware is ENERGIZED, safe_requested is false
  and teardown_started is false.
  Historical probe: docs/architecture/reviews/evidence/architecture_probe_20261031.c

Implementation task:
  Route active charging overflow through the coordinator's idempotent stop
  transaction after revoking permission. Preserve the resynchronization state
  and existing stop deadline/result. Fresh or late HLC status must not cancel
  the stop or restart transfer. Keep hardware command ownership in the
  coordinator and require measured safe-state confirmation for completion.
  Do not invent actuator retry policy or vehicle safety deadlines.

Acceptance criteria:
  - Overflow in READY_FOR_ENERGY and ENERGY_TRANSFER revokes permission before
    requesting coordinated safe stopping and arms bounded stop supervision.
  - Missing/delayed resynchronization cannot leave active charging outside a
    supervised stop transaction.
  - Repeated overflow is idempotent and preserves failures and deadlines.
  - Rejected/BUSY safe-state requests retain their result and reach the existing
    timeout escalation when measured safety is absent.
  - Fresh and stale status cannot revive a stopping session; completion requires
    current measured-safe evidence and the existing teardown conditions.
  - Add desired-behavior regressions for these cases and pass the portable suite.

[x] TASK-2 | P1 | Use trusted owner time for fault-triggered stop supervision | done
Status: FIXED — implementation 91cd37030d2af3c1c0a28616561ad34415e24704
Review finding: 2
Primary file: src/charging_coordinator/charging_coordinator.c
Related scope: coordinator public interfaces and affected callers/tests
Operations: OnHlcStatus, OnLinkStatus, OnHardwareStatus, Stop, Tick

Problem:
  Fault handlers pass producer envelope timestamps into Stop. Stop copies the
  timestamp into owner_time and arms the stop deadline from it. A future sample
  can make normal owner Tick calls return early and postpone timeout escalation.

Reproduction:
  Establish energy transfer at time 160, configure a rejecting hardware
  safe-state port, and inject a current-generation, higher-sequence HLC_ERROR
  with timestamp 1000000. With a synthetic stop budget of 100, owner_time
  becomes 1000000 and stop_deadline becomes 1000100. Tick(270) leaves STOPPING
  and simulated hardware ENERGIZED; timeout only latches at 1000100.
  Historical probe: docs/architecture/reviews/evidence/architecture_probe_20261031.c

Implementation task:
  Separate producer evidence time from trusted monotonic receipt/dispatch time.
  Provide trusted owner time to the affected ingress/control paths and use it
  for reactions and deadlines. Define future/stale evidence handling explicitly;
  invalid fault evidence must fail closed without moving or rewinding the owner
  clock. Preserve existing stop deadlines on repeated faults. Update affected
  PROJECT interfaces, callers and contract documentation as needed; preserve
  imported AUTOSAR ABI. Do not invent clock tolerances or normative deadlines.

Acceptance criteria:
  - Future-dated HLC_ERROR, PLC_DOWN and HW_FAULT cannot advance owner_time or
    defer a stop deadline beyond trusted receipt time plus the configured budget.
  - Delayed/reordered/invalid-quality samples cannot rewind owner time, extend
    an existing deadline, or revive a session; unrelated generations are rejected.
  - Normal owner ticks supervise rejected safe-state requests and missing
    measured confirmation at the configured deadline despite bad source times.
  - Repeated fault callbacks preserve stop idempotence, failure results and the
    original deadline.
  - Add desired-behavior regressions for all three ingress paths and pass the
    portable suite; run affected integration tests if interfaces change.

[ ] TASK-3 | P1 | Escalate an incomplete stop when measured-safe evidence expires
Status: OPEN
Primary file: src/charging_coordinator/charging_coordinator.c
Operations: Tick, OnHardwareStatus
Reviewed lines: 791-814 at c1c6b21

Problem:
  Tick's missing-measurement timeout checks the retained stop_measured_safe flag
  without rechecking hardware freshness. Once that flag is true, stale hardware
  evidence prevents the completion guard from passing but also bypasses timeout
  escalation when HLC/PLC teardown already completed. STOPPING can persist past
  its configured deadline without completion or a latched timeout fault.

Reproduction:
  Establish energy transfer at 160 using the review fixture. Select a synthetic
  hardware_max_age_us of 20 before Stop(170), with stop_timeout_us=100.
  Let the safe-state port return OK and both teardown ports complete.
  Supply newer measured SAFE/open/zero-current hardware at source/receipt 180.
  Call Tick(270): state=STOPPING (6), stop_measured_safe=true,
  stop_completed=false, stop_deadline=270, owner_time=270, fault.latched=false.
  The 180 sample is no longer current at 270. Repeated later Tick calls cannot
  complete or escalate this stop without additional external input.

Implementation task:
  Make completion and timeout supervision use current required measured evidence,
  rather than treating a retained boolean as indefinitely valid proof. At the
  existing deadline, an incomplete stop must reach the existing fault/escalation
  path if required completion evidence is missing or stale. Preserve the original
  deadline/result and command idempotence. Do not add actuator retries, assume
  measured safety from command acceptance, or invent a new physical deadline.

Acceptance criteria:
  - Fresh measured confirmation plus teardown still permits normal completion.
  - Expired measured-safe evidence cannot suppress timeout escalation or complete
    a stop; test a configured evidence age shorter than the stop budget.
  - Missing/invalid other required completion evidence cannot leave STOPPING
    indefinitely after its existing deadline without a defined terminal outcome.
  - No late evidence resurrects a fault-latched stop; commands remain idempotent.
  - Add desired-behavior tests separate from the dated review probe.

[ ] TASK-4 | P2 | Do not retain rejected CP/PP timestamps as ordering watermarks
Status: OPEN
Primary file: src/charging_coordinator/charging_coordinator.c
Operation: OnQualifiedCpPp
Reviewed lines: 95-101 at c1c6b21

Problem:
  The invalid CP/PP branch copies a future-dated envelope into cp_pp before
  rejecting it and clearing has_cp_pp. The next valid sample is rejected by
  obsolete() because its source timestamp is earlier than the rejected future
  timestamp, even when its sequence is higher. This blocks legitimate CP/PP
  recovery, including detection of a subsequent unplug, until that artificial
  future timestamp is reached or the context is otherwise reset.

Reproduction:
  Establish energy transfer at 160. Inject a current-generation, higher-sequence
  CP/PP sample with source timestamp 1000000 at trusted receipt time 170.
  It is rejected and starts stopping, but the cached timestamp becomes 1000000.
  Inject a valid same-generation UNPLUGGED sample with source/receipt time 180
  and a still higher sequence. It is rejected; cached timestamp remains 1000000,
  has_cp_pp=false and the retained plug state still indicates connected.
  The rejection is fail-closed for energy, but the valid unplug cannot progress
  the stop/recovery path. This is separate from TASK-2's three status APIs.

Implementation task:
  Keep current-generation invalid CP/PP evidence fail-closed without adopting its
  invalid timestamp/sequence as the accepted-sample watermark. Preserve unrelated
  generation rejection and trusted owner receipt-time semantics. A subsequent
  genuinely current, ordered sample must be able to restore source evidence or
  report unplug without reviving transfer in the stopping generation.

Acceptance criteria:
  - Rejected future/invalid CP/PP cannot poison the accepted ordering watermark.
  - Subsequent valid higher-sequence current CP/PP, including unplug, is handled.
  - Invalid evidence still revokes before coordinated stop during active charging.
  - Unrelated generations and genuinely reordered accepted evidence remain rejected.
  - Add desired-behavior recovery tests separate from the dated review probe.
